# Quantitative Foundations

These notes collect the foundational concepts needed to reason about statistical
models and inference. The collection is selective: it emphasizes ideas that
support later methodological work rather than attempting exhaustive coverage of
elementary statistics.

## Data and probability

- [Descriptive Statistics](/topics/foundations/descriptive-statistics):
  samples, populations, center, and variability.
- [Probability](/topics/foundations/probability): events, conditioning, and
  independence.

## Statistical inference

- [Sampling Distributions](/topics/foundations/sampling-distributions): how
  statistics vary across samples, standard error, and the central limit theorem.
- [Hypothesis Testing](/topics/foundations/hypothesis-testing): hypotheses,
  decisions, and error rates.
- [P-Values](/topics/foundations/p-values): tail probabilities and their
  interpretation.
- [Effect Sizes](/topics/foundations/effect-sizes): the magnitude of effects on
  original and standardized scales.
- [Statistical Power](/topics/foundations/statistical-power): sensitivity under
  a specified alternative.
- [Confidence Intervals](/topics/foundations/confidence-intervals): interval
  estimates, coverage, and precision.

## Tests for location

- [Sign Test](/topics/foundations/sign-test): an exact test based on signs and
  binomial tail probabilities.
- [t Tests](/topics/foundations/t-tests): one-sample, independent-samples, and
  paired comparisons of means.

A useful first path is descriptive statistics, probability, sampling
distributions, hypothesis testing, and p-values. Effect sizes and power then
connect the magnitude of an effect to study sensitivity. Confidence intervals
and the location tests combine these ideas in estimation and inference.
