# Psychological Measurement

**Canonical professional reference:** [AERA, APA, & NCME, *Standards for
Educational and Psychological Testing*](/resources#standards-for-educational-and-psychological-testing)
#ref/standards

Psychological measurement links a construct to observations through explicit
rules for eliciting, scoring, and interpreting responses. A useful instrument
depends not only on its items, but also on the intended score interpretation,
the population and use, the response process, and evidence about precision and
validity.

## Constructs, validity, and instrument design

## Score reliability and latent structure

- [Classical Test Theory](/topics/measurement/classical-test-theory): the
  observed–true–error model, test-family assumptions, measurement error, and
  attenuation.
- [Reliability](/topics/measurement/reliability): replication designs,
  reliability/precision, standard errors, test length, decision consistency,
  and reporting.
- [Internal Consistency and Coefficient Alpha](/topics/measurement/internal-consistency):
  alpha and its assumptions, Kuder–Richardson formulas, split-half estimation,
  and why internal consistency is not unidimensionality.
- [Factor Models and Reliability](/topics/measurement/factor-analysis):
  dimensionality, congeneric measures, omega, and maximal reliability.
- [Reliability of Change Scores](/topics/measurement/change-scores): when
  reliable measurements do and do not yield reliable differences.
- [Reliability of Composite Scores](/topics/measurement/composite-scores):
  weighted composites, Horst's formula, and stratified alpha.
- [Rater Agreement and Intraclass Correlation](/topics/measurement/rater-agreement):
  kappa, weighted kappa, and design-specific ICC estimators.

A productive path is to begin with the intended construct and use, design
items and scoring rules, examine item and response evidence, and choose
reliability or agreement analyses that match the design, while assembling
[validity evidence](/topics/measurement/validity) throughout. High reliability
alone establishes neither validity nor
[unidimensionality](/topics/measurement/factor-analysis).

For model-based alternatives, [Item Response Theory](/topics/irt) models item
responses as functions of latent traits, while [Cognitive Diagnosis
Models](/topics/cdm) classify attribute profiles. General [parameter
estimation](/modeling/estimation), [maximum likelihood](/modeling/maximum-likelihood),
and [EM](/modeling/em-algorithm) are canonical in the modeling section.
